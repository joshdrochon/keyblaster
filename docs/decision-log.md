### Learning engine
- **D17 · DECIDED · R2** Difficulty targets ~85% success (Wilson et al. 2019), treated as the center of an ~80–90% band, tuned in playtest.
- **D18 · DECIDED · R2** Difficulty increases only as the player gets better. Cold start handled by a disguised calibration/warm-up wave.
- **D19 · DECIDED · R4** Size/fall-time split. Asteroid size shows word length (motor cost, visible). Fall time is computed per player: (letters × keystroke budget from measured typing speed) + (recognition budget from that player's history with the word). Never shown as a rule. Grounded in LaBerge & Samuels (fluency = recognition latency) and Wilson et al. (85%).
- **D20 · DECIDED · R3** Controller knobs migrate from Type Storm's structure. Working rule: adjust one knob per stage.
- **D21 · DECIDED · R2** Speed tracks fluency; frequency tracks mastery. Weak-but-known words get time pressure; unknown words get more frequency and more time; mastered words become rare combo fodder.
- **D22 · DECIDED · R2** Every wave contains at least one guaranteed-catch word.
- **D23 · DECIDED · R2** Misses never cost story progress; a missed word comes back sooner.
- **D24 · DECIDED · R3** Auto-lock on first keystroke, no Enter. Wrong letter shakes, does not drop the lock. No switching targets once locked.
- **D25 · DECIDED · R4** Shared-first-letter asteroids are a mastery-gated difficulty tier (skinned as "dual cannons"). Lock resolves when the typed prefix is unique. Grounded in orthographic mapping (Ehri 2014).
- **D26 · DECIDED · R4** Shield canister ships. It carries a story word; tradeoff is which story word to prioritize, never a utility word.
- **D27 · DECIDED · R3** Hull: three hits per stage, full repair at stage end. Hits feed the stage star rating (informational feedback, Deci/Koestner/Ryan 1999).
- **D28 · DECIDED · R3** A missed asteroid visibly strikes the rocket: shake and spark, no explosion, no red flash.
- **D29 · DECIDED · R3** Empty hull = engine stalls out, mission fail. Restart from stage start.
- **D30 · DECIDED · R3** End-of-stage sentence is typed in a calm break (belt cleared, asteroids stopped). Blasted words highlighted; typos re-highlight the letter. Completing it activates the warp drive.
- **D31 · DECIDED · R5** The player should always feel like the best typer in the world; measured success ~85%, felt success near 100%. No red X's, no lives counter, no "wrong" sound.

- **D96 · RECOMMENDED · Sep 16 (build)** **Shape vocabulary is not what stands between this game and the Alto's Odyssey bar.** Two independent critics reviewed the eight generated silhouette sheets (`design-reference/refs/generated/`) — one given my diagnosis, one given nothing. Both rejected the sheets as a fix, and the one I had primed with my own "we generate geometry from noise, they author shapes" premise **refuted it**: fill authored massifs with one flat `#B5522A` and you get brown ribbons assembled from authored shapes instead of brown ribbons sampled from noise. The ribbon-ness comes from every layer sharing one fill value with no atmospheric lift, not from the contour being smooth. Evidence: Alto's own far-field mesas in `world-bar.png` are trapezoids with one step, and read as distance only because they sit near 90% of sky value at near-zero contrast; shape sophistication is concentrated in the few dark near layers where contrast is high. `WORLD-BAR.md` lists eight differences from the bar and these sheets address exactly one (item 5). **Order of work both critics converge on: (1) layer value ramp and atmospheric lift, (2) hue shift with depth — the one WORLD-BAR item never attempted, every sampled point in our Mars frame falls in a 16-degree hue wedge, (3) a genuinely dark framing foreground, then and only then (4) re-run the shape sheets.** Status RECOMMENDED because the direction question underneath it — whether a vertically-scrolling space game should carry terrain grammar at all — is the user's (see escalation E-world-5).

- **D97 · DECIDED · Sep 16 (user) · THE WORLD IS SPACE, NOT TERRAIN.** The parallax world drops terrain grammar entirely. No mesas, no landform bands, no horizon. Depth comes from things that do not need ground: ring planes seen edge-on, a planet limb, nebula bands, dust fields, debris at distance. Alto's Odyssey remains the bar for **value range, light, hue and restraint** — not for mesas (D59 is unchanged in intent and narrowed in reference). **The user made this call on sight**, zooming into the Title and asking why a structure's base was floating and then moving. It is not logical, and it never was: a landform band terminating in a dead-flat horizontal edge with sky visible underneath it, scrolling as a parallax layer. **Every measurement of the night converges on the same absence.** 81.4% of our upper-frame sky sits in one narrow brightness box against alto-03's 30.9%, because their sky is a third of the picture and land fills the rest; the art lane built a dark plane specifically to fix it and moved the number **one tenth of one percent** while re-creating the edge bars; a second coverage pass reached the same wall for the same reason (E-world-5b). Rejected: (a) give each stop a horizon — cheapest and looks right immediately on Mars and Jupiter, but impossible for Saturn's rings, Uranus, Neptune and the Kuiper belt, so four of seven stops would need a second grammar anyway; (c) ship as is — rejected because the user spotted the defect within seconds of looking, which is the only usability test that matters here. Supersedes the terrain half of **D86**/`art-direction.md` §2–§4; those sections are re-scoped to space forms. Closes **E-world-5** and **E-world-5b**.

- **D98 · DECIDED · Sep 16 (user) · SHADOW HAS ONE VOICE. THE SYSTEM VOICE IS CUT.** Web Speech is removed from the shipped build. **D88 is superseded**: it chose the browser's speech synthesiser as a *stand-in* because no `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` existed, and that condition ended — there is a key, and Shadow's lines are rendered with Liam (`TX3LPaxmHKxFdv7VOQHJ`, stability 0.92). The stand-in outlived its reason and stayed wired as the fallback under **every** speak site, so any line nobody remembered to render degraded silently to an OS voice instead of failing loudly. The user heard exactly that at the warp break and asked for it to be cut. **New rule: a spoken line without a rendered clip is a BUILD FAILURE, not a runtime fallback.** Silence is the runtime behaviour if one ever slips through — a missing voice is a small defect, the wrong voice in the middle of a children's game is a jarring one, and a build that cannot ship an unrendered line makes both unreachable. The coach note is included: `/api/coach` is not deployed, so D33's shipped fallback bundle plays every time, and 27 fixed strings were wrongly classified as unrenderable runtime text. Web Speech stays in the codebase behind an explicit opt-in for the case where a live LLM note is genuinely novel, and that path is off by default and covered by a test that proves it is off.

- **D99 · DECIDED · Sep 16 (user) · A SHORT TYPED LAUNCH CEREMONY AT EVERY STOP, AND IT RE-MEASURES.** `UR-28`: the typed launch sequence happens at the first stop and never again, and every later planet drops the player into the belt with nothing to type. Confirmed, and it was deliberate rather than a defect — `PreflightScene` gated the ritual on `newProfile || profileNeedsCalibration`, both of which are false for ever once a baseline is stored, so the first stop consumed D51's one ritual and stops 2–7 mounted a Pre-flight screen with `plan = null`. The source comment described that state as a costume with nothing underneath, which is an exact description of what `UR-28` reports seeing six times. **The options were put to the user and the choice made was a short ritual at every stop.** Earth keeps the full ~20 s measured ritual unchanged; every later stop gets a **launch ceremony**. **AMENDED Sep 17 (user), `UR-57`: the ceremony runs ALL THREE of D81's steps, each honouring its own declared `minWords`/`maxWords`, not two words on `systems`.** The original two-word figure was set in the brief and was wrong twice over. It overrode `systems`' own spec (3-4 words), and — the reason that decided it — the three steps measure DIFFERENT THINGS: `hull` contributes first-key latency and no intervals, `systems` and `engines` feed both, so a ceremony on `systems` alone collected about 6 intervals and exactly **2** word-start latencies. ## Type Storm reference (read from its shipped bundle, Sep 15 2026)

Their loop, so we can mirror the proven structure and improve on it.
- **Shield:** 3. Third breach ends the run. Breach = drone reaches 80% of screen height.
- **Wave config** (wave index w from 0): count = min(8 + 2w, 22); speed multiplier = 1 + 0.12w (uncapped); spawn interval = max(0.9, 2.4 − 0.14w) s, jittered ×0.75–1.25. Every 4th wave (w = 3, 7, 11…) is a Storm Titan boss.
- **Fall speed:** vy = (26 + rand·14) px/s × speed multiplier. On a ~790px-tall viewport, wave 1 drones take ~16–25 s to breach; by wave 5 (speed 1.6) ~10–15 s.
- **Estimated wave lengths:** wave 1 ≈ 20–35 s (8 drones spawning over ~19 s), wave 2 ≈ 25–40 s (10), wave 3 ≈ 25–40 s (12), boss ≈ 10–20 s. Three waves + boss ≈ 1.5–2.5 min.
- **Word tiers:** waves 0–1 Home Row (as, ask, dad, fall, flask…), 2–3 Short Words (and, art, box, cat…), 4–5 Everyday (apple, brave, cloud…), 6+ Power Words (adventure, brilliant, challenge…). Pool = all tiers up to current, current tier weighted 3×. Consecutive picks never share a first letter.
- **Boss sentence:** picked at random from five pangrams unrelated to the waves: "the quick brown fox…", "pack my box with five dozen liquor jugs", "how vexingly quick daft zebras jump", "sphinx of black quartz judge my vow", "the five boxing wizards jump quickly". This confirms the original critique (sentence disconnected from the words typed) and, notably, ships the word "liquor" to grades 3–12 — the exact guardrail example in D34.
- **Scoring:** word = length × 20 × combo (combo capped ×10); boss = 250 × √(sentence length) × combo. WPM = chars/5 ÷ minutes. Accuracy = hits ÷ (hits + typos).
- **Speed is a single global knob, no per-player adaptation.** Our D19/D53 replace this.
